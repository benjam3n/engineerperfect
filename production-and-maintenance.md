# Production and maintenance

A successful instance establishes that its particular route worked. Reproduction requires a route for additional instances under the variation and resource demands of their production. Continued usefulness requires a route for replacing what use consumes.

If production of one acceptable unit consumes r units of an unreplenished stock B, then no arrangement respecting that consumption can produce more than floor(B/r) units. Describing the unit more accurately does not change the bound. Recycling, substitution, a new supply, or a different production mechanism can change the premises and therefore the capacity.

This distinction matters for an intellectual method too. A response obtained with an expert repeatedly repairing hidden omissions has consumed an expert contribution. Removing that contribution while preserving the claimed capability requires another mechanism to supply what it supplied. Saving the final response does not save the expert's discriminations, access, and decisions unless those contributions become available in a usable form.

Suppose each usable result requires e > 0 units of that expert's intervention, and only B units are available. Without a substitute or replenishment, the same stock bound permits at most floor(B/e) results. Calling the method general or complete does not remove its repeated support cost. A reusable correction changes this arrangement only when it supplies the missing discrimination or operation where the next result is produced. A stored warning elsewhere can leave the original consumption unchanged.

## The maintainer is part of the operating arrangement

Let an asset provide k service events before a repair is needed. Suppose each service and each repair uses one unit of the same exclusive labor resource. Over a long run, k services require at least one repair. The resource demand per service is at least 1 + 1/k labor units, ignoring startup credit and any additional overhead.

For k = 2, the asymptotic throughput of one unit of labor per period is at most 2/3 service events per period. More assets can improve timing and provide startup stock; they cannot remove this long-run labor inequality while all other premises remain fixed.

In the [two-station example](examples/service-during-repair.md), every period requires a completed service. There is no spare period for repair. One worker must eventually fail despite having two stations. Two concurrent workers make a sustainable alternating operation possible. The missing contribution is repair labor at the right time, not awareness that maintenance matters.

If upkeep is outsourced, it remains a dependency of the system that receives it. Its price, availability, response time, tools, and information may determine whether the guarantee survives. Moving the work across an organizational boundary does not remove it.

This determines the scope of the inquiry from the promised result. A professional may be assigned only the analysis, but a claim that the arrangement can continue must include the repair contribution or remain conditional on it. The [perspective critique](unoptimized-perspectives.md) identifies the source-specific distinction between a profession's remit and the causes on which realization depends.

## Reserves determine how long repair may take

Suppose an interruption consumes a usable buffer at rate d > 0 and repair takes time τ, with no replenishment during repair. A buffer B preserves service through the interruption exactly when B ≥ dτ under this constant-rate model. With uncertain demand d ≤ d_max and repair duration τ ≤ τ_max, B ≥ d_max τ_max is sufficient and necessary if that worst-case pair is admitted.

If demand and repair duration cannot jointly attain their separate maxima, the exact requirement is B ≥ max(dτ) over the admitted joint set. Replacing that set by independent intervals can overstate required reserve. Ignoring their dependence can also understate it when favorable averages conceal a long high-demand interruption.

The remedy can be a larger buffer, lower demand during interruption, faster repair, or an alternative live route. Their costs differ. “Increase resilience” leaves those alternatives unconstructed; the equation supplies a concrete comparison once their values are known.

## Reproduction can fail through a hidden one-time contribution

Suppose a tool requires a specially fitted part. The prototype received one from its inventor; the published production procedure begins after that part already exists. The procedure can be accurate for its stated input while failing to reproduce its own starting conditions.

The [tooling example](examples/tooling-and-production.md) derives a bootstrap operation, includes its extra cost, and recalculates the production threshold. Adding the missing contribution changes the preferred investment from two units to four units. Omitting it would recommend construction too early.

A production recipe must therefore account for the facilities that produce its inputs at the claimed scale. It need not recreate the whole universe. It must identify which prerequisites the actual undertaking can depend on and which it is responsible for supplying.

## Reliability and recoverability answer different questions

A system that rarely fails and cannot be repaired differs from one that fails more often and recovers quickly. If the only recorded measure is failures per operating hour, the time unavailable and the consequences of each interruption remain undetermined.

Under a stipulated alternating cycle of uptime U and downtime D, the long-run available fraction is U/(U+D) when those cycle lengths are fixed. Reducing D can improve availability without changing U. Improving U can leave unacceptable recovery times unchanged. A real random process needs its own assumptions before an analogous estimate is used.

The relevant perfection claim may require both no violation of a critical condition and an adequate service fraction. Averaging cannot turn a prohibited event into compliance. Nor does identifying a prohibited event determine the appropriate requirement for every other service. Each demand must retain its actual meaning.
